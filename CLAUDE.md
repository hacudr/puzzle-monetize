# Puzzle app

A picture-puzzle web app for iPad (plain HTML/CSS/JS, no build step). This folder is the version to be sold, kept at https://github.com/hacudr/puzzle-monetize; the free preview is hosted on GitHub Pages from `main` of a second repository, `hacudr/puzzle`, at https://hacudr.github.io/puzzle/.

The home page is a gallery of periods; tapping one opens a page of that period's artists, and tapping an artist opens a page of their puzzles. Each puzzle in `puzzles/puzzles.json` names its `artist`, and `puzzles/artists.json` lists the artists in the order their cards appear, each with a `note` (the italic line, such as "French, 1840–1926"), a `period` and a `cover` (one of their puzzle images). `puzzles/periods.json` lists the home page's categories in the order their cards appear, each with a `note`: Intro to Art History first, then Female Artists, then the periods. Intro to Art History is the ten puzzles that are free, being marked `"free": true`; it names them itself, in a `puzzles` list, and a category that does so has no artists and opens straight on those pictures, in that order, each of which stays under its own artist as well. A category's card shows its first artist's cover, unless the category names a `cover` of its own, as Female Artists does. A woman artist also carries `"also": ["Female Artists"]`, so she appears there as well as under her period. The home page can be switched from Categories to Artists, which lists every artist on a card without a picture, in the order of their surnames; the choice is remembered. Beside those two, Surprise Me (`surprise` in `app.js`) opens a puzzle drawn by chance from the unsolved pictures the settings leave on show, or from all of them once every one is solved, when it is dealt afresh. The surname is the last word of the `name`; where that is wrong, as in Rembrandt van Rijn or Hudson River School, give the artist a `sort` word. Keep every category to at most 10 artists, splitting one that grows past that; an artist with no `period` lands under "Other artists".

Every other picture is locked until the app is bought (`locked` in `app.js`): one purchase, at $9.99, opens every picture; there is no buying an artist or a category alone. A locked picture's card carries a padlock and "Upgrade" in its top left corner, and a tap on it brings up the sheet that offers the purchase; the cards for artists and categories carry the padlock alone while anything behind them is locked, and still open to be looked through. Next Puzzle passes the locked pictures over, and Surprise Me draws only from pictures not locked, so before the purchase it keeps to the free ten, and after it to all of them, the settings still hiding what they hide. Solving the last of the free ten brings up the same sheet over the finished picture, headed "Unlock all puzzles" (`paywall`), and Next Puzzle brings it up again from any free picture while it has not been bought; paying goes straight on to a puzzle (`onward`): the first category after Intro to Art History, its first artist and their first puzzle not yet solved, whose way back leads to that artist's page and the category. Not Now leaves the solved screen with its ways back to the gallery. In Kids mode the sheet shows no price. Settings has Restore Purchases. `store.js` keeps whether the app has been bought (`puzzle.owned`) and is the one place a shop is to be wired in, at `pay` and `fetchOwned`; none is yet, so a purchase goes through only on localhost, for trying the locks (`store.forget()` in the console locks everything again), and is refused anywhere else. The lock is drawn by the page and no more than that: every picture is still a file anyone can fetch, until the app is sold through something that checks.

Each time the app is opened afresh it starts on a splash: one picture filling the screen under a title card, "Welcome to Art History · Best played with music", and "Tap to begin", put away with a tap. With the music on, that tap starts `music/welcome.mp3`, the opening of Debussy's Clair de lune (a CC BY recording, so the splash names its player under the picture's title), which plays softly over the gallery for thirty seconds and then dies away over five; it dies away sooner, over a second and a half, when a puzzle is opened. It waits for the tap because a browser allows no sound before the first touch. The picture is never one with a `content` tag, whatever the settings show, and each opening takes one not shown before until every such picture has had a turn. A reload in the same tab does not bring the splash back.

Every button answers a tap with the stroke of a paintbrush on canvas, under half a second of soft noise with no pitch to it (`sound.tap()`, made by the app itself and so no file with rights to clear). Settings has two sound switches. Sound effects covers this tap sound, the notes the pieces play as they fall into place, and the swords, hooves and knocking at the end; Music covers the splash's music and the music that ends a solved puzzle. The music's own on/off button and Off for Kids mode stay silent. On an iPhone the audio can die while the app is put away or the screen locked, and no setting is to blame: every touch calls `sound.unlock()`, which replaces an audio context that has run and since been away, or whose clock has stopped, with a new one (and fetches again what the open puzzle ends on). It has not been tried on a phone, only reasoned from how iOS behaves; if sound is lost again, that is where to look.

A puzzle that has been solved opens from its card on the finished picture in its frame, not on the pieces, and Solve Again deals it afresh; one begun again and left half done opens where it was left. On Expert that takes having solved it in black and white. The bar at the top of the gallery's pages, with the way back and the settings, stays put while the page scrolls under it (`.top`). Every card crops its picture to the same upright shape, so each puzzle's card carries a small outline in its top corner of the picture's own shape, tall, wide or square (`markShape` in `app.js`, `.shape` in `style.css`), drawn once the thumbnail is in.

A solved puzzle hangs in a carved, gilt frame, a photograph cut out of its background: `frames/gilt-frame.webp`, and the same frame turned on its side, `frames/gilt-frame-landscape.webp`, for a picture wider than it is tall. It carries a fine brown outline round the carving, drawn into the file, with 8 clear pixels on each side to hold it. The frame is drawn in nine pieces, so its corners keep their shape and only the runs between them stretch to the picture; the widths of its sides are set on `.plate` in `style.css`. Settings has a Frame choice between this one, Ornate, and Minimalist, the plain gilt edge the styles draw by themselves (`.frame`, put on by `.plate.plain`); the choice is remembered, and Ornate is the usual one. The photograph is the user's own, as `frames/CREDITS.md` records; a frame from anywhere else needs its photographer's CC0, a frame not being a flat work.

## Rights: everything must be fit for a paid app

The app is to be sold to users in the United States. Assume that of every file that ships with it: pictures, music, sound effects, fonts and icons. Nothing goes in unless it may be used commercially, and whatever a licence asks for by way of credit is given inside the app, not only in the repository. When the rights to something cannot be shown, leave it out and say so.

**Artwork** must be in the public domain in the United States. That means the artist died more than 70 years ago and the work was first published more than 95 years ago (before 1931, as of 2026); for an artist who died in the last hundred years, check both. A museum that releases a work as open access or CC0 has made that check already.

**The file** of a picture has rights of its own, apart from the painting's. Take only:

- a file a museum releases as CC0 or open access (the National Gallery of Art, the Met, Cleveland, the Art Institute of Chicago, the Rijksmuseum), or
- a file on Wikimedia Commons marked "Public domain" or CC0, being a plain, square-on reproduction of a flat work.

Never take a file under a licence with NC or ND in it. Do not take CC BY or CC BY-SA pictures either: the app has nowhere to credit a photographer. A photograph that shows the frame, the wall or the room, or a work that is not flat (a sculpture, a painted shield), is the photographer's own work and needs their CC0, whatever the age of the thing shown; a thin edge of frame or stretcher on a CC0 file may be cropped off in the puzzle copy. Do not download from a museum's own site when its terms forbid commercial use; they bind as a contract even where no copyright exists.

**Record the source.** Every picture has an entry in a file beside the originals, `puzzles/images/<surname>.<source>.json` (`nga`, `met`, `commons`, or `user` for a photograph the user took themselves, square-on and without the frame), giving the page it came from and its licence. A picture with no such record has not been cleared.

**Italy.** Italian law asks for permission and a fee for commercial reproductions of works in Italian state collections, public domain or not. It has no force in the United States, which is one reason the app is sold there only; the Commons record marks such pictures with `italianHeritageNotice`. Raise it with the user before the app is offered in Italy or the rest of Europe.

**Music and sounds.** The composition must be in the public domain, and the recording public domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA, never NC or ND. Each recording is listed in `music/CREDITS.md` with its player, licence and source, and one under CC BY or CC BY-SA carries its `licence` in `CLIPS` in `sound.js`, so that the solved screen names who is playing. A sound effect needs the same record of where it came from, kept in `sound-effects/CREDITS.md`; the three there now are the user's own recordings.

**Fonts** must allow being bundled in a paid app (the SIL Open Font Licence does), with the licence text kept beside them.

Not yet cleared, to be settled before the app is sold:

- The pictures by Michalina Janoszanka (died 1952, so the 95-year test matters), Virginia Frances Sterrett, the Hudson River School and all but one by Artemisia Gentileschi have no record of where their files came from.
- Joseph Stella died in 1946, so the 95-year test matters. That each picture was published before 1931 rests on its Commons tag alone, and the Met, Chicago and Cleveland mark their own Stellas as not in the public domain. Two are weaker still: Purissima, which Commons tags as unpublished, and Coney Island, which the Met gave to Commons as CC0 but no longer lists as open access.
- `music/avemaria.mp3` is marked Public Domain at the Free Music Archive and CC0 at Commons, but the file there carries an older tag reading CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, and Commons has not reviewed the licence. The page's word is the later one; it wants confirming.
- The solved screen names the player of a licensed recording but not the licence, which CC BY and CC BY-SA ask to be named and linked. The app needs a credits page for that.
- `fonts/` has no copy of the Open Font Licence that Spectral and Averia Serif come under.

## When the user adds images

The user drops original pictures into `puzzles/images/` and says little more than "I added some images". Whenever there are pictures in `puzzles/images/` that are not yet in `puzzles/puzzles.json`, do all of the following for each one without being asked and without asking first:

1. **Puzzle copy** — write a web-sized copy to `puzzles/<slug>.webp` (lowercase, hyphenated name, starting with the artist's surname, as in `monet-the-japanese-footbridge.webp`), longest side at most 2048 px. That is as large as an iPad can show; anything bigger only costs download time. WebP at quality 75 is a little over half the size of the JPEG and looks the same. Never modify or delete the originals in `puzzles/images/`.

   ```bash
   ffmpeg -y -v error -i "puzzles/images/<original>" -vf "scale='if(gt(iw,ih),min(2048,iw),-2)':'if(gt(iw,ih),-2,min(2048,ih))':flags=lanczos" -c:v libwebp -quality 75 -compression_level 6 "puzzles/<slug>.webp"
   ```

2. **Thumbnail** — write a copy whose shorter side is 600 px to `puzzles/thumbs/<slug>.webp`, using exactly the same file name as the puzzle copy. (The cards are upright and crop to fill, so a wide picture needs its height, not its width, to be 600.) The collection cards load this; a missing thumbnail falls back to the full picture, which is slow on the iPad.

   ```bash
   ffmpeg -y -v error -i "puzzles/<slug>.webp" -vf "scale='if(gt(iw,ih),-2,600)':'if(gt(iw,ih),600,-2)':flags=lanczos" -c:v libwebp -quality 75 -compression_level 6 "puzzles/thumbs/<slug>.webp"
   ```

   Before either copy is made, be sure the picture passes the rights section above, and that its source and licence are on record. One that does not pass gets no puzzle; tell the user which and why.

3. **Puzzle entry** — look at the picture, then add an entry to `puzzles/puzzles.json` with the same fields the existing entries use (`image`, `artist`, `name`, `scientific` for the italic line, plus any optional fields the neighbouring entries carry, such as `music`: `moonlight`, `clair`, `pathetique`, `arabesque`, `flaxen`, `reflets`, `lente`, `gondola1`, `gondola2`, `gondola3`, `gnossienne` or `gnossienne2` (the same piece's second theme); a puzzle without one takes the next tune in that order by its position in the list, unless its `content` has `violence` or `frightening`, when it takes the next of the stormy tunes, which are played on the lute where the others are played on the piano: `summer`, `diesirae`, `strife`, `lamentoso`, `mountainking`, `baldmountain` or `newworld` (Wagner's Ride of the Valkyries was taken out at the owner's wish and is not to come back); every tune ends on a few seconds of a recording kept in `music/`, whose sources and licences are in `music/CREDITS.md`). Give the entry `"sacred": true` if its subject is religious: a scene from the Bible, a saint, a Madonna, a prophet. Such a picture takes the next of the sacred tunes in place of the piano ones (`kyrie` and `avemaria`, in turn; any tune in `sound.js` marked `sacred` joins them), unless its `content` makes it stormy, when it keeps the stormy tunes. A church seen as a building, a churchman's portrait and a figure from myth are not religious pictures. Give the entry `"effect": "sword"` only if the picture is one of violence or menace in which a sword has a part, a battle, a killing or a threat with a sword in view, so that a clash of swords from `sound-effects/Sword.m4a` sounds with the ending music. Such a picture carries the `violence` tag below. A sword that is only worn or held, as in a portrait, a knight at rest or a saint with the sword that is their sign, does not count, nor does a dagger, a lance or a commander's baton, nor a violent picture in which no sword can be seen. Give it `"effect": "gallop"` if the picture shows a horse, ridden, harnessed or loose, so that hooves from `sound-effects/Gallop.m4a` sound there instead; a horse too small to pick out does not count. Give it `"effect": "knock"` if the picture shows Christ knocking at a door or standing by one, as in The Light of the World, so that three soft knocks from `sound-effects/Door knock.m4a` sound there. A picture with more than one takes a list, as in `"effect": ["sword", "gallop"]`. Say which titles, dates or attributions are guesses. If the artist is new, add them to `puzzles/artists.json` too.

   Give the entry a `content` list if the picture shows any of these, and leave it out otherwise: `nudity` (any visible nudity at all, including the naked infant in a Madonna and Child, cherubs and nude statues), `violence` (fighting, weapons in use, wounds, blood, killed animals, martyrdoms, abductions, menace against women, as in Susanna and the Elders, and any reference to rape, in the title or the story the picture tells, however calm the picture looks, as in Lucretia or The Feast of the Gods) and `frightening` (monsters, menacing beasts, corpses, human skulls, people in peril). Settings can hide each kind, and Kids mode hides all three, so when unsure, tag it. Say which pictures were tagged.

4. **Check** — load the gallery in the preview and confirm the artist's card is there, every card on their page shows its picture and the new puzzle opens. Tap the splash away first. Kids mode must be off for this (Settings, hold Off for three seconds), or tagged pictures will be missing.

Thumbnails are part of adding a puzzle, never a separate request. If any listed puzzle is missing its file in `puzzles/thumbs/`, generate it.

## Pulling pictures from the National Gallery of Art

`node nga.js "Claude Monet"` fetches every open-access picture by that artist from the gallery's open data into `puzzles/images/` (4096 px on the longest side, as JPEGs named ready to use as the slug) and writes `puzzles/images/<surname>.nga.json` with the gallery's title, date, medium, credit line and a description of each picture. It skips pictures already downloaded. A second word keeps to one kind of work, for an artist the gallery also holds hundreds of prints by: `node nga.js "Mary Cassatt" Painting`. Then carry out the four steps above for each one, taking titles and dates from that file rather than guessing.

## Ancient art from the Met

The Ancient Art category has no artists: its cards are cultures, Ancient Egypt, Ancient Near East, Ancient Greece and Ancient Rome, each standing as the `artist` of its puzzles, and the slugs start `egypt-`, `near-east-`, `greece-` and `rome-` in place of a surname. Every one is from the Met's Open Access (CC0), recorded in `puzzles/images/<culture>.met.json`; the Met's CC0 covers its photographs, so a statue, vase or room may go in. Seven of the Egyptian puzzles are not ancient paintings but the copies of them that the Met's Egyptian Expedition painted in the tombs in 1908–1930s (Nina and Norman de Garis Davies, Charles K. Wilkinson), and their italic line says so; Nina Davies died in 1965 and Wilkinson in 1986, so they pass the 70-year test only because the Met, which made them, releases them as CC0. Its departments are Egyptian Art (10), Ancient West Asian Art (3) and Greek and Roman Art (13). The Met's API retired `/public/collection/v1/search` on 1 October 2026; search with `/public/collection/v1.1/search` (`q`, `departmentId`, `isHighlight`, `hasImages`, `limit`, `offset`), and ask slowly, a second or so apart, or its firewall answers with a page of HTML.

Anglo-Saxon and Saxon Art is made the same way, from the Met's Medieval Art and Cloisters departments: two cards, Anglo-Saxon England (slugs `anglo-saxon-`, the Met's culture "Anglo-Saxon", c. 450–1066) and Saxony (slugs `saxon-`, works the Met places in Saxony or Lower Saxony, or Ottonian, the Saxon emperors' art, to 1270), recorded in `puzzles/images/anglo-saxon.met.json` and `saxon.met.json`. The Met holds few Anglo-Saxon works with pictures, about twenty, so that card has nearly all of them.

Japanese Prints is ukiyo-e from the Met's Asian Art department (CC0): ten artists from Harunobu to Yoshitoshi, five or six woodblock prints each, recorded in `puzzles/images/<surname>.met.json` and slugged by the art name the prints are known by (`hokusai-`, `hiroshige-`), which is also the last word of the `name`. The Met holds many impressions of the same print; take its own photograph of the one with the best colour, and leave out a photograph that shows a colour bar or a ruler, or crop it to the sheet and say so in the record, as was done for Yoshitoshi's Teasing the Cat. Leave out the Met's early 20th-century recuts (marked so in their dates), pillar prints too narrow to make a puzzle, and shunga.

## Pulling pictures from Wikimedia Commons

For an artist whose paintings hang where there is no open-access programme, as Caravaggio's do in Italian churches and museums. Wikidata lists an artist's paintings with the Commons file of each (`wdt:P170` for the artist, `wdt:P18` for the picture, `wdt:P373` for the Commons category, which often holds a larger file); the Commons API (`prop=imageinfo&iiprop=size|extmetadata|url`) gives each file's size and, in `LicenseShortName`, its licence. Keep only files marked "Public domain" or CC0 whose longest side is at least 2048 px. Download the original, or the 3840 px rendering (`iiurlwidth=3840`) of one larger than 5000 px, into `puzzles/images/` under the slug, sending a User-Agent that names the app. Write `puzzles/images/<surname>.commons.json` with each picture's `file`, `title`, `date`, `collection`, `wikidata` id, `source` (the Commons page), `licence`, `size` and `italianHeritageNotice` (whether the page carries the Italian heritage template).

Then look at every picture before carrying out the four steps: leave out black-and-white photographs of lost paintings, files that show a frame or a room, and pictures doubtfully attributed. Commons gives no dependable titles or dates, so take the usual scholarly ones and say that they are such. Where two pictures share a title, start the italic line with the city, as in "Paris, c. 1595–1598".

## Publishing

- Push to GitHub only when the user asks. Both repos are public, so everything pushed is public. The pages at GitHub are a free preview of what will be sold, so the rights section applies to them in full.
- There are two remotes. `origin` is `hacudr/puzzle-monetize`, where a plain `git push` goes; it has no GitHub Pages. `preview` is `hacudr/puzzle`, which serves the pages; push there (`git push preview main`) only when the user asks for the preview to be brought up to date, a push to `origin` not being such a request.
- `puzzles/images/` (originals), `dist/`, `mockups/` and `.claude/` are git-ignored on purpose.
- After a push to `preview`, GitHub Pages takes a minute or two to update.

## Running locally

`node serve.js` serves the app on port 8000 (the preview uses `.claude/launch.json`).
